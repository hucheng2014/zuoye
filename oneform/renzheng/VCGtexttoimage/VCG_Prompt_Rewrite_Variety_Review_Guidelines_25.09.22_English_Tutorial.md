# VCG Prompt Rewrite Variety Review Guidelines 25.09.22 — English Tutorial

## 1. Task Goal

This project evaluates the quality of **four images generated from the same input prompt**. Unlike single-image evaluation, you must assess both:

1. **Individual image quality** — whether each image is structurally sound and aligned with the prompt.
2. **Set-level usefulness** — whether the four images provide meaningful visual alternatives rather than near-duplicates.

The main grading dimensions are:

- **Structural Integrity**
- **Text-to-Image Alignment**
- **Variety** — a new dimension for this project

> Structural Integrity, Text-to-Image Alignment, and the final ranking process should be used together with the general **Image Evaluation Guidelines**. This tutorial focuses on the project-specific rules for evaluating four images as a set, especially Variety.

---

## 2. Recommended Workflow

### Step 1: Evaluate each image individually first

For each of the four images, check whether:

- the image has distortion, artifacts, missing parts, extra parts, malformed objects, or unreadable text;
- the main subject, action, scene, attributes, and relationships match the prompt;
- the issue level is minor, noticeable, or severe.

### Step 2: Assign set-level grades

After reviewing all four images, assign an overall grade for:

- Structural Integrity;
- Text-to-Image Alignment;
- Variety.

### Step 3: Rank the outputs

Use the general Image Evaluation Guidelines > Ranking Scale section. Ranking should consider:

- how consistently the images maintain structural quality;
- whether all images satisfy the prompt;
- whether the set provides meaningful and diverse options for the user.

---

## 3. Structural Integrity

### 3.1 What to evaluate

Structural Integrity measures whether the image itself looks visually coherent and technically sound. Common issues include:

- malformed object shapes;
- distorted, missing, or extra body/object parts;
- blurry or broken text;
- visible artifacts, melting, tearing, or unnatural blending;
- physically implausible details, such as a straw disappearing under water or an unnatural spoon shape.

### 3.2 Set-level grading rules for four images

| Overall Rating | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Very Poor** | **Three or more** images have Severe or Noticeable Structural Integrity issues. |
| **Poor** | **One or two** images have Severe or Noticeable Structural Integrity issues. |
| **Good** | No Severe or Noticeable issues; the set may contain only Minor Structural Integrity issues. |
| **Very Good** | All four images have no Structural Integrity issues, distortions, or artifacts. |

### 3.3 Example interpretations

- **Very Poor**: Most shampoo bottle shapes do not make sense, or three images show noticeable issues such as straws disappearing under water.
- **Poor**: Two images show unnatural straws/spoons, or two images contain blurred words.
- **Good**: Only a small issue appears, such as a distorted cinnamon stick or an unnatural cat tongue while the other images are structurally strong.
- **Very Good**: All four images have high structural integrity with no visible distortion or artifacts.

---

## 4. Text-to-Image Alignment

### 4.1 What to evaluate

Text-to-Image Alignment measures whether the output satisfies the input prompt. Check whether the images correctly represent:

- the main subject;
- quantity;
- action;
- attributes such as color, shape, material, expression, or pose;
- the required scene or background;
- important constraints or modifiers in the prompt.

### 4.2 Set-level grading rules for four images

| Overall Rating | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Poor** | **Three or more** images have alignment issues. |
| **Neutral** | **One or two** images have alignment issues. |
| **Good** | All images are highly aligned with the prompt. |

### 4.3 Example interpretations

- **Poor**:
  - Prompt: “cloud in the shape of a heart.” All images fail to look like a heart-shaped cloud.
  - Prompt: “mouse with a balloon tied to its tail.” None of the images show the balloon tied to the mouse’s tail.
  - Prompt: “mac and cheese.” Two images do not clearly look like mac and cheese, even if the general idea is somewhat recognizable.
- **Neutral**:
  - Prompt: “table and chair.” One output shows a sofa instead of a chair; it is somewhat acceptable but still partially misaligned.
- **Good**:
  - Prompt: “tiger playing with a toy airplane.” All images match the prompt.
  - Prompt: “wine bottle with a cheese board.” All images include the wine bottle and cheese board correctly.

## 5. Variety — New Dimension

### 5.1 Definition

Variety refers to the **visual and conceptual differences** among multiple images generated from the same prompt. Differences may appear in:

- composition;
- viewpoint;
- pose;
- background;
- atmosphere;
- added attributes;
- style;
- alternative visual interpretations of the same prompt.

High Variety means the images provide clear, meaningful, and distinct interpretations. It does **not** mean random or irrelevant changes.

> If a prompt is very specific and restrictive, lower variety may be expected. Do not penalize outputs for staying close to a highly constrained prompt.

### 5.2 Distinctiveness scale

| Rating | Description |
|---|---|
| **Low Variety** | The images repeat the same concept. Differences are barely noticeable or limited to slight color, background, or viewpoint changes that do not meaningfully affect the reviewer’s experience. |
| **Moderate Variety** | Some differences are present, but the variations are limited in scope or depth. The images may still share similar compositions or visual structures. |
| **High Variety** | Each image presents a clearly different visual interpretation. The changes are substantial and meaningful, such as different compositions or visual structures. The set avoids redundancy and provides visually diverse options. |

### 5.3 Creativity scale

| Rating | Description |
|---|---|
| **Low Variety** | The outputs feel flat, predictable, and overly literal. They offer no alternative styles or sense of play/surprise. |
| **Moderate Variety** | The outputs show some imaginative thinking but do not fully explore creative possibilities. They are not completely repetitive, but they do not strongly surprise the reviewer. |
| **High Variety** | The outputs go beyond straightforward interpretations and visualize the prompt from different angles, providing reasonable and valuable surprises. |

### 5.4 Variety examples

| Prompt | Rating | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| a bowling alley | Low | The concept repeats; only the number of pins or minor details change. |
| cactus wearing sunglasses | Moderate | Some variety is visible, but background and pose variation remain limited. |
| a satellite dish on a roof | High | Roof styles and backgrounds vary; the night-sky image adds surprise. |
| tandoori chicken with naan bread | Low | The outputs repeat the same layout and provide little distinctiveness. |
| three finger salute | Moderate | The main difference is background color, which adds little creativity. |
| stop sign | High | The designs vary meaningfully from image to image. |
| river and bridge | Low | The outputs are basically identical and provide no useful variation. |
| saw and wood | Moderate | Angles and colors change slightly, but the set still has room for improvement. |
| window and curtain | High | Curtain colors and views outside the window vary richly. |

## 6. Practical Grading Advice

### 6.1 Judge usability before variety

If most images have serious structural or alignment problems, do not over-reward the set just because the images look different. A good order is:

1. Structural Integrity;
2. Text-to-Image Alignment;
3. Variety;
4. Final ranking.

### 6.2 Variety is not minor visual noise

The following usually do **not** justify High Variety:

- changing only the background color;
- slightly moving the subject;
- using nearly identical composition with a tiny viewpoint change;
- repeating the same object while changing only the count;
- making changes that do not alter the viewer’s understanding or experience.

### 6.3 High Variety must still respect the prompt

Strong variation should:

- remain aligned with the prompt;
- provide different compositions or conceptual interpretations;
- avoid random unrelated additions;
- make the user feel they received four valuable options.

---

## 7. Quick Checklist

Before submitting, confirm that:

- [ ] You reviewed all four images individually.
- [ ] You identified Structural Integrity issues per image.
- [ ] You counted how many images have Severe or Noticeable Structural Integrity issues.
- [ ] You counted how many images have Text-to-Image Alignment issues.
- [ ] Variety was judged by meaningful differences in composition, background, pose, visual structure, or concept—not just color changes.
- [ ] You considered whether the prompt itself limits possible variety.
- [ ] Your final ranking combines structure, alignment, and variety.
