Guidelines                Structural Integrity refers to the anatomical correctness and visual integrity of subjects and objects in a generated image. Evaluators
                          determine whether each element is formed correctly and consistently, based on the prompt—considering both explicitly stated and implied
Prompt Analysis           anatomical or structural details.
                         • Structural Integrity should be judged without factoring in any other dimensions.
Flags                    • Correct Parts and Proportions: Unless otherwise specified by the prompt, subjects should have the typical number, proportions, and
                           arrangement of their constituent parts.
Grading Dimensions           ◦ Humans: The front view of a human head should typically show two eyes, a nose, a mouth, and two ears. If the body is visible, it usually has
                               two arms with hands and two legs with feet. Pay particular attention to the eyes to ensure there are no issues with gaze or placement.
Visual Quality
                             ◦ Animals: For instance, a tiger would normally have four legs, a tail (depending on the viewpoint), sharp teeth, and claws.

Text Quality                 ◦ Objects: Buildings usually display roofs, windows, doors, etc. Cars have four wheels, windows, and doors. Airplanes have two wings, engines,
                               etc.
Structural Integrity         ◦ Text: Any text appearing in the image should be clearly legible and free of errors. Text should read as meaningful language rather than
                               gibberish or random characters, and it must not contain spelling or grammatical mistakes.
Input/Output Alignment   • Prompt-Relative Evaluation: Structural integrity is judged based on what the prompt requests or implies, not on a general standard of "real-
                           world" accuracy. A three-headed dog is structurally sound if the prompt asks for a three-headed dog.
Style Alignment
                             ◦ Explicit vs. Implied Features:

Diversity Evaluation            ▪ Explicit: Features directly mentioned in the prompt (e.g., "a blue car with five wheels").
                                ▪ Implied: Features not directly mentioned, but reasonably expected based on common understanding (e.g., "a man" implies a human form

Ranking Scale                      with two arms, two legs, etc.).
