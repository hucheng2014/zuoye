It seems graders are evaluating better sounding rewrites higher
but the output should not be rewritten. If any of the models
introduces edits that do not fix objective mistakes, the response
should be graded as partially following instructions. In particular,
even if the source prompt includes factually incorrect information,
the response should *not* correct the factuality of the source.
Moreover, note that rewrites that change the style of the source
prompt should also be marked as partially following instructions;
this includes "sanitizing" regionalism by replacing them with
standard Italian.

Grader quality
issue

SAA feedback: the purpose of "following instructions" it is not
clear to analysts. In the links provided, they have a tendency to
makr as "fully following" those responses in which a prompt is
rewritten with a better grammatical structure rather than factual
mistakes. For examples, in tasks with scientific prompts, they
tend to grade higher those responses that correct the scientific
facts, misinterpreting the goal of "proofreading".
The point that seems clearer to AAs is that, if the prompt doesn't
contain major mistakes and the response simply rewrites the
original text with no modifications, that is to be considered fully
following.
In their defense, the "Following Instructions" section of the
guidelines should be more direct and specific on what's the goal
of the project. The addiction of the examples surely helps, but an
expansion of the definition of that dimension could be of further
benefit.
Also, the definition of "fully, partially and not" following
instructions in the UI are very vague and do not give much insight
on what is expected from the project.
