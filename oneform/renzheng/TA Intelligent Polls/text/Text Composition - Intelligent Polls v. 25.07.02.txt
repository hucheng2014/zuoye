Introduction

The goal of the Intelligent Polls feature is to 1) assess whether it is appropriate to have a poll based on user's conversation and 2) to generate
one if it is appropriate. A valid poll for a conversation requires an intent to gather opinions on a specific shared activity or event, aiming for
consensus among participants.


A good poll should contain a title and multiple, unique options grounded in the input text. In some circumstances, it is appropriate not to
generate a poll. For example, when users already reach a consensus, seek advice or recommendations from other users, no poll is appropriate.


This is an addendum to the response evaluation in the Preference Ranking Guidelines. It highlights crucial guidelines in Proper no reply,
Following Instructions, Composition, Comprehensiveness, Groundedness, and Satisfaction.
Workflow
1. Review the Input Text


2. Assess Whether the Response Meets the Proper No Reply Requirements


•   Refer to the Proper No Reply Assessment section.
•   Proceed to Step 3. Evaluate the Response if a poll is generate. If no poll is generated (blank response), end the
    task in this step.


3. Evaluate the Response
•   Refer to the sections of the other evaluation dimensions.
Section 1:
Proper No Reply
Proper No Reply
Overview
After reviewing the input text, the first evaluation asks you to evaluate whether the response meets the Proper no Reply requirement.


You will answer this question: Should there be a poll after the preceding conversation?
    Proper No Reply
    Overview
    Scale for Single Response Rating


    This dimension has two rating scales:


    • No poll is appropriate: There should be no poll because a poll is not appropriate based on the context. Examples include: when a
       consensus has already been reached; when a user is seeking advice on complex or personal matters; when users are discussing personal
       preferences and are not aiming to reach consensus.
    • Poll is appropriate: There is an intent by at least one participant to gather opinions on a specific shared activity or event, aiming for
       consensus among participants.



                 For a Single Blank Response


                 If a task shows an empty single response (i.e. no poll is generated):
                  •   You will only have to assess whether the empty response meets the "Proper No Reply" (no poll is appropriate)
                      requirement.
                  •   You won't have to evaluate all the other remaining questions. Please go ahead and submit the response.

Section 2:
Following Instructions
        Following Instructions
        Overview
        The Following Instructions dimension is to check if the text assistant follows the instruction to assess the context and generate (or not generate)
        suggested responses (viewed as poll candidates in this task).


        It is important to note that Following Instructions should not be mistaken for accuracy or correctness. Inaccurate responses should only be
        penalized under Groundedness or Comprehensiveness dimensions, not under Following Instructions.




            Key Callout
         It s crucial to recognize that in certain situations, no poll may be appropriate. If you have selected No poll is appropriate for the previous
         question and the model generates an empty response, it would be seen as Following.
􀁞
    ’
Following Instructions
Scale for Single Response Rating


• Following: the response has to meet all of the requirements below to be rated as Following
     ◦ Your answer to the previous Proper No Reply question is "Poll is appropriate."
     ◦ A poll was generated, with a title and 2 or more unique options.
     ◦ OR, you have selected "No poll is appropriate" for Proper no Reply and the response is empty.

• Not Following: the response doesn't meet the requirements for Following. For example:
    ◦ A poll is generated when it should not be.
    ◦ No poll is generated when there should be one.
    ◦ A poll is generated, but it misses a title, or omits one or more options, or contains repeating options.

Section 3:
Composition
    Composition
    Overview
    The writing quality of a poll should be good. This means that it is semantically coherent and consistent with the conversation. The poll title and
    options should be concise (as opposed to verbose), free of grammatical errors, not awkward-sounding, and coherent.




            When there's a typo in the preceding conversation: If the conversation contains a typo and it can be logically inferred what the
         user intended to say, the generated poll should correct the typo.

           • If the poll corrects the typos: Good Composition, if the poll is semantically coherent and consistent with the conversation.
           • If the poll doesn't correct the typo: Bad Composition, given that it is considered a grammatical error.
􀁞
Composition
Scale for Single Response Rating

• Good: the response must meet the requirements below to be rated as Good.
    ◦ The poll title and options are well-composed.The poll title and options are well-composed.
    ◦ The poll title and options are concise, error-free, and sound natural.
    ◦ The poll title is a short phrase which describes the purpose of the poll--not a complete sentence or a question.
    ◦ The poll title and options are semantically coherent and consistent with the conversation, showing a correct understanding of it.
• Bad: The poll title or options are not well-composed. They sound awkward, contain grammatical errors, or show poor semantic understanding of
  the conversation..

Section 4:
Comprehensiveness
Comprehensiveness
Overview

To be comprehensive, a poll must 1) include all poll options that are explicitly mentioned in the preceding conversation, and 2) those options
must appear in the same order in which they were first mentioned.
Comprehensiveness
Scale for Single Response Rating


• Comprehensive: the response must meet the following criteria to be considered comprehensive:
  • The poll includes all unique options that are explicitly mentioned by participants in the preceding conversation.
  • The poll options appear in the same order as which they were first mentioned in the preceding conversation.

• Not comprehensive: The poll does not include all unique options but omits or repeats some of them.
    Comprehensiveness
    Scale for Single Response Rating



              Important
         When a poll includes a previously rejected option:
          ◦    Not Comprehensive: if a poll includes an option that was mentioned but later rejected, please mark the poll as Not
               Comprehensive, because the option wouldn't considered as "explicitly mentioned."
          ◦    Comprehensive: In contrast, if the poll correctly excludes this rejected option, please mark it as Comprehensive.


         When a poll makes up a new option in addition to previously mentioned ones: You may see cases in which a poll not only
         includes all previously mentioned options in the right order (Comprehensive), but it also adds a new option that didn't
         appear in the preceding conversation. Under this circumstance, you don't need to penalize the poll under Comprehensiveness
         because it already meets the requirements for this dimension.
              • Instead, this scenario speaks to a Groundedness issue. Please penalize the poll under Groundedness.
              • See example 2 below.

Section 5:
Groundedness
    Groundedness
    Overview

    To be rated as Grounded, a poll must have a title that is grounded and only include options which are explicitly mentioned in the preceding
    conversation. That is, it must not include any option that does not appear in the conversation.




            If a poll omits an option but all of the other suggested options are grounded in the input text, the response would still be considered
        “Grounded.” You would panelize a response that omits options under “Comprehensiveness.”
􀁞
Groundedness
Scale for Groundedness Rating

• Grounded: The poll title is relevant to the conversation (i.e. the model didn't hallucinate and make up a title), and the poll only includes options
  that are explicitly mentioned by participants in the preceding conversation.


• Not grounded: The poll includes an irrelevant title or options that are not mentioned by participants.

Section 6:
Harmfulness
Harmfulness
Overview

Familiarize yourself with the Safety Evaluation Guidelines categorization of Harm and guidance to effectively identify, rate and categorize harmful content.




It's essential to remember that while the assistant's primary role is to fulfill user requests helpfully, ensuring safety and preventing harm is paramount.
When evaluating for potential harmfulness, the emphasis should be on the response's harmlessness, rather than the degree of helpfulness it
provides to the user's request.

Harmful requests and responses manifest in various ways, please refer to the Safety Evaluation Harmfulness Guidelines for details.
Harmfulness

of harm or the scale of its impact.
