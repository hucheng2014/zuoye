Introduction
Feature Overview
The Mail Smart Reply feature is designed to help users draft emails more efficiently. When a user replies to an email or
composes a new email, the feature analyzes the user profile and suggests a complete, ready-to-send draft.
The goal is to provide drafts that are not only accurate and contextually appropriate but also sound authentically like the user
who is sending them.

Grading Task
Your task is to evaluate the quality of AI-generated emails to ensure they are helpful, accurate, safe, and personalized.
Depending on whether the user is composing a new email or replying to an input email, you will be presented with a
primary input email, a user prompt, user profile, and suggested replies.

Additional Personal Info
In some cases, you will also see Additional Personal Information displayed for you.
• The field displays personal information or context that the model is able to retrieve from the user's device.
• Additional Personal Information field will mainly impact your assessment of Groundedness.
• Important: In the same task, you can expect to see one response has Additional Personal Information, while the others
don't. The Additional Personal Info is specific to each response, not the task as a whole.
• When the prompt and additional personal information conflict: If the prompt (and previous email, if any) contradicts
the additional personal information, prioritize the prompt and previous email across all dimensions when evaluating the
response.
• For example, if the prompt says "Schedule a meeting with Octavio" but the additional personal information shows a
meeting is already scheduled, a response that proceeds to schedule the meeting should be evaluated as adhering
to the instruction / fitting context.

Groundedness
⚠ Groundedness Decision Rules
To align with engineering intent, it's important to understand what counts as "reasonable" and "grounded" for this
feature. When evaluating Groundedness, apply the rules below:
Rule 1: Named entities must be verifiable; non-named invented specifics may result in a Partially Grounded
response.
If the draft names a specific entity — a place, person, restaurant, park, city, date, or time — check whether it appears in
the instruction, additional personal information field, prior email, or a verifiable source (e.g., web search result).
If it cannot be verified, and considering the content of the draft aligns with the core message, rate Partially Grounded.
For example:
• "Brunch at the Riverside Bistro at 11am" → Partially Grounded (unverifiable name and time)

Instruction Adherence & Contextual Fit
Grading Question and Scales
Q. Does the suggested draft follow the user s instruction and fit the email context (if any)?
• Followed and Fit: The draft accurately interprets and applies the user's instruction, preserving intended meaning,
priorities, and constraints, AND appropriately addresses the previous mail's key points (if any), maintaining thematic
coherence and matching the expected formality level. Minor stylistic variations that don't affect the core instruction are

• Partially Followed and Fit: The draft generally follows the instruction and fits the context, but has minor deviations — such

from the previous mail. The primary intent is recognizable and largely fulfilled.

intended meaning, OR (if a previous mail exists) fails to address the recipient's key points, introduces irrelevant content, or
mismatches the appropriate formality level for the context.

Appendix

Grading Question and Scales
How to evaluate the Personalization dimension?
It is acknowledged by the engineering team that this dimension can be subjective and context-based. To guide your
grading, we have added the grading principles below:

⚠ Personalization Dimension - Grading Principles
• Guidance, Not Law: Consider the guidelines for this dimension as a framework to inform your decisions, not
strict mandates. Human communication is fluid, and our goal is to capture its nuances.
• User Profile as a Tendency: The user profile (capitalization, punctuation, emoji use, abbreviations, etc..)
describes what a user often does, not what they always do. It's "including but not limited to" these elements.
Look for patterns, but recognize exceptions.
• Context is Key: The relevance of specific profile components (e.g., emoji vs. capitalization) is dynamic and
depends entirely on the conversation's context. Use your common sense and the provided examples to weigh
which components are most significant for the task at hand.
