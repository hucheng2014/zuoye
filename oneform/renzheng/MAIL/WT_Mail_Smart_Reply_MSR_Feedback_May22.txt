Objective
The goals of this document are:
•
•
•

Understand error trends across dimensions and locales
Identify root causes driving annotation mistakes
Define action plan for calibration and quality improvement

Error Breakdown
1. Personalization
This is the dominant issue across almost all locales (DK, DE, NO, EN-GB, FR, IT, JP, KO, NL, PT, SE, VN, ZHHK, ZH-TW). Inconsistent grading between Personalization categories:
•
•
•

Guidelines misunderstanding: Drafts appropriately adapt to the specific email context and
relationship even though it deviates from the user profile, warranting Contextually Adapted over
Generic or Personalized which is explicitly explained in the guidelines.
Over-penalization: There are a few tasks where annotators penalize drafts as generic or
mismatch even though they follow relationship context, mail patterns, and user profile.
Excessive padding: Responses are flagged as “overly padded” adding filler phrases that inflate
length beyond what the user’s typical style would suggest.

Trend: Calibration gap – guidelines not consistently internalized across annotators. Graders tend to
score this dimension subjectively rather than following guidelines.

2. Groundedness
This was reported across all locales, and mainly split between three causes:
•
•
•

Under-penalization: Annotators overlook unconfirmed or newly introduced information,
incorrectly grading the responses as “Grounded”.
Over-penalization: Annotators overlook that additional information appears in only one
response, resulting in a false “Not Grounded” instead of “Grounded”.
Inconsistent annotation of what counts as grounded: Annotators disagree on whether
reasonable cultural inferences or polite additions qualify as grounded or partially grounded –
these are Grounded and shouldn’t be penalized.

Trend: The annotators tend to overlook key details in prompts, contexts, or reference emails, resulting
in both under- and over-penalization. Surface-level check makes annotators miss key information from
Additional Personal Information box.

3. Localization
This issue has been reported in several cases for (DK, NO, DE, FR, IT, KO, NL, ZH-CN, ZH-HK, ZH-TW):
•
•
•
•

In both Danish and Norwegian, a comma should not be inserted after the opening or sign-offs of
an email; the line spacing serves as the separator. The annotators either did not know this rule
or missed the error.
Language mixing and wording not common for specific locale overlooked by annotators.
Localization issue has been reported where there weren’t any.
Machine-translated phrasing: Unnatural or stilted expressions that feel like direct translations
rather than native writing, even when technically grammatically correct.

Trend: Annotators are either overlooking punctuation errors or lack awareness of locale-specific
formatting rules. For FR-FR and NL-NL, cases show that punctuation errors and scrambled symbols got
overseen and not penalized.

4. Instruction Adherence & Contextual Fit
This dimension was flagged for (DK, DE, NO, TR, EN-GB, FR, IT, JP, KO, NL, PT, SE).
•
•
•

Wrong name addressed in draft – name could be found in user profile so Instruction Adherence
& Contextual Fit should be rated Partially Followed and Fit
Over-penalization: Responses that were fully compliant got graded as Partially Followed and Fit
Under-penalization: Annotators show a tendency to default to "Followed and Fit" when
"Partially Followed and Fit" would be the correct rating

Trend: Miscalibration around the "Partially Followed and Fit" threshold, annotators both under-apply it
by defaulting to "Followed and Fit" despite wrong names or missing elements and over-apply it by
downgrading fully compliant responses.

5. Pairwise Comparison
Trend: Annotators Pairwise Comparison was consistent with their own SBS results, but because it was
built on incorrect grading in the previous dimensions, the QAer’s Pairwise Comparison led to a different
conclusion.

6. Naturalness
Although low in overall frequency, this dimension demonstrated consistent deviations for (DK, NO, TR,
FR, IT, JP, KO, NL)
•
•

Over-penalization of naturally written responses: Annotators tend to incorrectly mark fluent,
contextually appropriate responses as Unnatural despite the language reading like authentic
human communication.
Under-penalization: Genuinely awkward or robotic responses passed as Natural.

Trend: Annotators tend to mistake minor formatting issues for bad fluency and over-penalize good
responses, while on a few occasions letting genuinely awkward writing slide.

7. Tone and Empathy Alignment
This dimension got flagged for (NO, FR, NL) in isolated cases, and logged errors were:
•
•

Under-penalization: Responses with a tone that mismatched the expected formality of the
context were marked as Aligned when they should have been Not Aligned.
Over-penalization: Responses that fully match the required tone and empathy level were
incorrectly downgraded as Not Aligned.

Trend: Inconsistent assessment of tone fit: Annotators tend to overlook inappropriate formality level.

Root Cause Analysis
•
•
•
•
•
•

Subjective Personalization grading: Annotators rely on personal judgment instead of guidelines.
Surface-level Groundedness review: Annotators aren’t consistently cross-referencing all
available fields before grading, leading to miss actual hallucinations in some cases, and falsely
flag valid information in others.
Overlooking specific locale formatting rules: Annotators lack awareness of punctuation
placement in emails or missed language mixing which must be failed.
Pairwise comparison: Side-by-side comparison gets incorrectly graded due to overlooked issues
in previous dimensions.
Isolated Instruction Adherence oversights: Errors are rare and non-systemic, limited to human
errors.
Isolated Tone and Empathy Alignment: Annotators lacked consensus in isolated cases whether
drafts were aligned or not.

Action Plan
Annotators must calibrate against given examples in guidelines before assessing Personalization
•
•

Calibrate application of the Personalization dimension against Step 6 Suggested reply 1 and 2 in
the guidelines, analyzing the variance between Personalized and Generic
Ensure alignment with the Contextually Adapted framework under the query in “Q. How well
does the suggested reply reflect the user’s communication style?”

Annotators must systematically cross-check text details rather than relying on a surface-level review
before assessing Groundedness
•
•

Cross-check all information available in the task to validate that the response does not
hallucinate, preventing the under-penalization of unconfirmed details
Ensure to check additional personal info in all responses so a response is not falsely flagged as
“Not Grounded”, avoiding improper over-penalization

If a name is mentioned in the profile, instruction, previous email - it is still grounded in the

Annotators must enforce regional formatting conventions and rules before finalizing Localization

Verify locale-specific punctuation constraints, ensuring that no commas are placed after email
greetings or sign-offs in da_DK and nb_NO. (This should also be added to locale-specific rules in

Screen carefully for unauthorized language mixing and unnatural phrasing to ensure they are

Validate that localization error genuinely exists before downgrading

Severity Calibration
Re-establish clear thresholds with concrete examples across the most impacted dimensions:
Personalization
•
•
•

Personalized: The draft reflect the user’s unique communication style: reads as authentically
from the user.
Contextually Adapted: The draft intentionally change aspects of the user’s typical style to ensure
the response remains appropriate for the specific email context.
Generic: The draft fails to incorporate the user’s writing style patterns and mail component
patterns when they would be appropriate for the context.

Groundedness
•
•
•

Grounded: When person name that can be derived from the task’s (user prompt, previous
email, additional info, or profile).
Partially Grounded: Draft that gets the core message right but adds a minor invented detail.
Not Grounded: A draft that introduces invented fact that changes the meaning of the message.

Centific Confidential ©

•

Calibration note: Make sure to systematically check Additional Personal Information for both
drafts.

Localization
•
•

Issue: Scrambled symbols and formatting rules that are locale-specific. (Wrong punctuation
format).
No issue: Grammar/Spelling do not fall under Localization issue.

Instruction Adherence & Contextual Fit
•
•
•

Followed and fit: The draft follows all elements of the instruction. Minor stylistic differences
allowed as long as it preserves the intended meaning.
Partially Followed and Fit: The draft mostly fits the email context but has a minor formality
mismatch. The draft responds to incorrect name.
Calibration note: Make sure the draft is evaluated against previous mail if there is any, and make
sure there’s no secondary element that gets omitted (Partially Grounded).

Pairwise Comparison
•
•

The annotator’s Pairwise Comparison aligned well with their own SBS result.
Calibration note: Misgrading early dimensions creates an error effect of the final comparison.
