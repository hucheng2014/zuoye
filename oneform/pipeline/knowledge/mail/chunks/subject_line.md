# Subject Line

## Purpose
If the task requires evaluating the email subject line, assess its quality against the following standards.

## Subject Line Quality Rules

### 1. Format: Topic + Action Noun
The subject line should follow a "Topic + Action Noun" structure.
- Good: `关于域名续期及潜在合作机会` (topic: domain renewal, action: partnership opportunity)
- Good: `Re: Meeting Schedule Confirmation`
- Bad: `Hi there!` (no topic or action)
- Bad: `A very long subject line that tries to summarize the entire email content in one sentence` (too long, not concise)

### 2. Title Case Rule (Language-Dependent)
- **English**: title case may apply (evaluate if present)
- **Chinese and other non-English locales**: title case does NOT apply
- **CRITICAL: Never check "Uses title case" for Chinese (zh_CN, zh_TW, zh_HK) subject lines.** This is a hard rule.
- Similarly, do not apply English title case expectations to Danish, Norwegian, or other non-English locales

### 3. No Trailing Punctuation
- No period at the end of the subject line
- No exclamation marks
- No question marks
- No emoji

### 4. Tone and Content
- Must be neutral and professional
- Should convey exactly one main idea
- Should not be sensational or clickbait-like
- Should accurately reflect the email content

## Pairwise Consideration for Subject Lines
When comparing two responses' subject lines:
- Both follow format rules -> equal on this dimension
- One violates format rules (trailing punctuation, wrong title case, etc.) -> penalize that response
- Subject line quality feeds into the overall pairwise comparison but is not the sole deciding factor
